🤖 AI Prompt: Case Law Summarisation

Eddie's Method (Coaching Session 2 of 6)

← Back to Hub
💡 Use this to process ANY case law, journal article, royal commission report, or case study. Read the AI summary FIRST, then read the actual case.

The Prompt (Copy-Paste Ready)

I am going to give you a legal case. I want you to summarise the case for a first-year law student studying at an Australian university. In your summary, I want you to identify and clearly structure the following: 1. Case Citation - Full case name and citation in AGLC4 format - Court and jurisdiction - Year decided 2. Administrative/Procedural History - How did this case get to this court? - What happened at first instance? - Was it appealed? From where? - What orders were made previously? 3. Parties - Who is the plaintiff/appellant/applicant? - Who is the defendant/respondent? - Brief description of each party 4. Judicial Members - Which judges/justices heard the case? - Who wrote the majority judgment? - Were there any dissenting or concurring judgments? By whom? 5. Detailed Facts - What actually happened? Tell me the story in plain language. - Include all material facts (facts that affected the legal outcome) - Chronological order where possible - Be thorough: I need to understand the full factual matrix 6. Legal Issues - What legal questions did the court need to decide? - List each issue separately - For each issue, note which area of law it falls under 7. Arguments - What did each side argue? - Structure as: Plaintiff/Appellant argued... / Defendant/Respondent argued... - Include key submissions and reasoning behind them 8. Holdings/Decisions - What did the court decide on each issue? - Was the appeal allowed or dismissed? - What orders were made? 9. Ratio Decidendi (The Rule) - What is the legal principle established or applied by this case? - State it as a clear rule that could be applied to a new set of facts - This is the most important part 10. Obiter Dicta (If Significant) - Any important comments not strictly necessary for the decision but influential 11. Key Paragraphs - Identify paragraph numbers containing: the ratio/key rule, the test, key factual findings, any defences discussed - Format as: [Paragraph X] contains... 12. Relevance - Why is this case important? - What area(s) of law does it primarily apply to? - Is it still good law?

How to Use

  1. Copy the prompt above
  2. Paste into your AI tool (ChatGPT, Claude, etc.)
  3. After the prompt, paste the full case text OR say: "The case is [Name] [Citation]. Please summarise it."
  4. Read the AI summary first to understand the case
  5. Then read the actual case yourself (you'll know what to look for)
  6. Verify the AI's key paragraphs against the real case (AI hallucinates)
  7. Extract the rule/principle and slot into your notes

Variations

For Journal Articles

  • Citation (AGLC4)
  • Author background
  • Thesis/argument
  • Methodology (if empirical)
  • Key findings
  • Supporting evidence
  • Counterarguments addressed
  • Conclusions
  • Relevance to your unit
  • Key quotes with page numbers

For Legislation

  • Full title and citation (AGLC4)
  • Jurisdiction and commencement date
  • Purpose/objects of the Act
  • Key definitions
  • Relevant sections (plain-language)
  • Tests established
  • Steps to apply each test
  • Defences or exceptions
  • Penalties or remedies
  • Key amendments or regulations

For Royal Commission Reports

  • Full title and citation
  • Commissioner(s)
  • Terms of reference
  • Key findings
  • Recommendations relevant to your topic
  • Evidence cited
  • Legislative or policy changes that resulted

Eddie's Rules for AI Use

⚠️ The Legal Admissions Board does NOT penalise AI use for research. The line is using it to WRITE your actual submissions. Stay on the right side.
  1. Never use AI to write your assignments (research and synthesis only)
  2. Always verify against the primary source (AI hallucinates case law)
  3. Use AI to locate specific paragraphs
  4. Use AI for AGLC4 checking on your referencing
  5. Use AI for feedback on draft arguments and structure
  6. Read the case AFTER the AI summary so you're not seeing it cold
  7. Everyone is using AI for research: not using it means falling behind

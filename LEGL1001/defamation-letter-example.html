LEGL1001 Task 4 Example | Professional Legal Correspondence

Context

This is an example of a letter of advice from a solicitor to a client regarding a potential defamation claim. It demonstrates professional legal writing, application of the Defamation Act 2005 (NSW), and practical client advice.

RE: ADVICE – DEFAMATION

I am writing to you to confirm what we discussed in our conference on 1 July 2016. You sought advice about a possible defamation claim against your local supermarket, Westward.

You told me:

  1. As you were shopping for your groceries, a security person from Westward came up to you and the following conversation took place:
    Security Person: "Come with me. You have cold meat stashed inside your coat pockets" or words to that effect.
    You: "What?"
    Security Person: "You have stolen from the supermarket. Follow me to my office where you can wait for law enforcement to arrive."
  2. A small crowd then formed and you noticed a few people from your neighbourhood. Everyone was staring at you. You felt humiliated.
  3. You followed the security person back to his office and you said to him "my shopping basket was full and the items I put in there were falling out so I put them in my coat pocket. I intended to pay for them with my other items at the checkout".
  4. He didn't seem entirely convinced by he let you go with a warning saying "I'll be watching you".

You believe that you have been defamed as a result of this incident.

💡 Structure note: The letter opens with context (conference date, what client told the solicitor) before moving to legal analysis. Facts are presented as the client reported them.

THE LAW ABOUT DEFAMATION

The Law that governs defamation in NSW is the Defamation Act 2005 ('The Act'). Section 3 of the Act defines one of the objectives to be providing appropriate and fair remedies for those who reputations have been harmed because they have been publicly defamed. There is no longer any distinction between saying defamatory things or writing defamatory things.

One of the other objectives is to resolve matters in a timely and non-litigious manner.

Section 12 of the Act encourages people to resolve matters out-of-court by making amends. Another section, Section 20 encourages remedies such as making an apology. As a first step, a letter could be sent to Westward to advise them of your complaint and request that they apologise to you.

I know that you told me that you want to go to court to clear your good name but because these matters are heard in the Supreme Court of NSW and can become very expensive in a short period of time. Even if you are able to establish that Westward defamed you, they will then most likely raise a defence, including that it was true, that it was an honest and reasonable opinion.

If you are unsuccessful with you claim, you may then have to not only pay your legal fees, but you may also be ordered to pay Westward's legal fees.

Therefore I recommend that a letter be sent to Westward seeking an apology as a first step, before any further action is taken. You should take some time to consider the information in this letter before you decide what you would like to do.

If you would like the letter to be sent, please contact me and I will provide you with a draft letter for your approval before it is sent.

I look forward to hearing from you.

Yours Faithfully,

R. Humphrey

  • Empowers client to make their own decision
  • Professional format and sign-off

Aggravated Assaults, Consent & 'Acceptable Violence'

Problem Question + Detailed Notes

Problem Question

Dwayne arrives home from work to find someone attempting to break into the shed behind Dwayne's home (where Dwayne keeps his large collection of power tools). The man (Argo) is holding a short-handled sledge-hammer and is trying to smash the very large padlock on Dwayne's shed. Dwayne shouts 'What the hell are you doing?!', and walks towards Argo. When Dwayne is about a metre away, Argo says, 'Back off!' and swings the sledgehammer in Dwayne's direction. Dwayne sways just in time to avoid being hit, but in so doing, he stumbles, loses his balance and falls to the ground. Dwayne's head hits the corner of a step that leads up to the garden, and he is knocked unconscious.

Argo drops the sledge-hammer, runs to his van which is parked nearby, jumps in and drives away.

X-rays reveal that when Dwayne's head hit the step, he suffered a hairline fracture to his skull. An MRI shows no evidence of brain injury. Dwayne is released from hospital two days later. Apart from regular headaches for another two weeks, Dwayne suffers no long-term physical effects of his injury.

Argo is arrested and charged with causing grievous bodily harm with intent to cause grievous bodily harm (Crimes Act 1900 (NSW), s 33(1)(b)).

You are an ODPP solicitor. You have been asked to advise on the strength of the case against Argo, and whether an alternative charge should be preferred.

1. Assault Causing ABH (s 59)

s 59(1) Whoever assaults any person, and thereby occasions actual bodily harm, shall be liable to imprisonment for five years.

s 59(2) In the company of another person: liable to 7 years.

ConductCircumstanceConsequence
ARAssault (any sort), VoluntaryNilABH
MRIntention OR recklessnessNANil

Key Points

  • ABH = "Any hurt or injury calculated to interfere with the health or comfort" of person; more than merely transient or trifling (Donovan)
  • Includes psychiatric injury (Chan-Fook) but NOT emotions like fear or panic (Lardner)
  • No need for specific intent to cause ABH (Coulter); recklessness sufficient (Williams)
  • Recklessness = foresight that actions WOULD effect unlawful contact (Coleman, MacPherson)

3. Causing GBH (s 54)

s 54 Whosoever by any unlawful or negligent act, or omission, causes grievous bodily harm to any person: imprisonment for two years.

  • GBH s 4 CA: destruction of foetus, permanent/serious disfiguring, grievous bodily disease
  • "Really serious" (DPP v Smith); need not be permanent (Haoui); must be physical (Swan)
  • More than simple fracture needed (Kbayli)
  • NOT GBH: no displacement, no operative treatment, no permanent injury, short hospital stay (Swan)
  • MR: intention to commit unlawful act OR gross negligence (D [1984])

4. Reckless Wounding (s 35(4))

s 35(4) Wounds + reckless as to causing ABH. Max: 7 years.

Wounding = incision or puncture breaking the dermis (Shepherd). Epidermis only is insufficient. Split lip sufficient.

5. Recklessly Causing GBH (s 35(2))

s 35(2) Causes GBH + reckless as to causing ABH. Max: 10 years.

6. Intentional Wounding/GBH (s 33(1))

s 33(1) Wounds or causes GBH with intent to cause GBH. Max: 25 years.

Rule: Prosecution must prove D caused GBH + had specific intent to cause GBH. This is the hardest charge to make out.

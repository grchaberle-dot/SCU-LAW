2020 Torts Exam Hypothetical

Jacinda's Housing Problem - Melbourne Law School

The Problem

Jacinda owns a house which she rents out. She has engaged a property manager, Kev, to manage the property on her behalf. Kev's responsibilities include finding tenants, collecting rent, and arranging for any necessary maintenance and repairs.

The current tenants are Boris and his family. Boris has complained to Kev on several occasions about a large tree in the backyard that appears to be dying. Boris is worried that the tree might fall on the house or on his children's trampoline. Each time, Kev has told Boris that he will "look into it" but has done nothing.

One night during a severe storm, a large branch from the tree falls onto the roof of the house, causing significant damage to the roof and allowing rain to enter the house. The rainwater damages Boris's expensive home entertainment system, which is worth $15,000.

Boris's daughter, Cindy (aged 8), is frightened by the noise of the branch hitting the roof. She runs outside in a panic and trips on the uneven path in the backyard (which Boris had also previously complained about to Kev). Cindy breaks her arm.

Meanwhile, Jacinda's next-door neighbour, Donald, has been conducting noisy renovations on his property for the past 6 months. The noise occurs every day from 7am to 6pm, including weekends. The renovations involve heavy machinery and loud music played by the workers. The noise is so bad that Boris cannot work from home (he is a freelance writer) and his family cannot enjoy their backyard. Boris has lost approximately $20,000 in income because he cannot concentrate on his writing.

Donald's builders have also been parking their trucks on the street in front of Boris's house, making it difficult for Boris to access his driveway. On one occasion, a builder reversed a truck into Boris's car, causing $3,000 damage.

Advise Boris as to any tortious claims he may have, and against whom.

Issues to Consider

  • Negligence: Kev/Jacinda re: tree branch (duty, breach, causation, damage)
  • Negligence: Kev/Jacinda re: uneven path (Cindy's injury)
  • Occupier's liability: obligations to tenants
  • Vicarious liability: Jacinda for Kev's failures
  • Private nuisance: Donald's renovations (noise, enjoyment of land)
  • Public nuisance: trucks blocking street access
  • Trespass to goods/negligence: truck reversing into car
  • Pure economic loss: Boris's lost writing income
  • Remoteness: Cindy running outside in panic

Source: Melbourne Law School 2020 Torts Examination

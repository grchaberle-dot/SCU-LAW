Liberalism: Core Characteristics

↓

Equality
↓

Rule of Law

Liberalism emerged from the Enlightenment. It replaced divine authority with reason, individual autonomy, and limited government. It is the dominant ideology of Australian law.
Negative Liberty: Freedom FROM interference (Locke, Mill)
Positive Liberty: Freedom TO do things, state enables opportunity (T.H. Green, communitarianism)
The individual is the fundamental moral, social, legal unit. Society = collection of autonomous rational beings. Thatcher: 'there's no such thing as society'.
Formal: Like cases treated alike (procedure)
Substantive: Equality of outcome (addresses structural disadvantage)
Deontological (Kant): Rights exist by virtue of being human. Cannot be used as means to an end.
Teleological (Bentham/Mill): Rights justified by consequences. Greatest good for greatest number.
Government bound by law. Separation of powers (Legislative/Executive/Judicial). Laws: predictable, non-retroactive, clear, stable.
JS Mill: Even if all mankind minus one held one opinion, silencing that one person is not justified. Freedom of speech, thought, action.

Thin vs Thick Rule of Law

Thin / Formal (Raz)

  • No moral judgment on content
  • Supremacy of law
  • Equality before law
  • Laws: general, promulgated, clear, prospective, stable
  • Politically neutral
  • More focused
  • Morally neutral value of law

Thick / Substantive (Bingham)

  • Must protect fundamental human rights
  • Access to justice
  • Fair procedures
  • State comply with international law
  • Practical: procedures alone don't protect against tyranny
  • Symbolic: oppressive regimes shouldn't get 'rule of law' label

Legal Formalism vs Legal Realism

Formalism

  • Law = self-contained system
  • Judges apply rules mechanically
  • No external considerations
  • Promotes predictability
  • Democratic legitimacy (deference to legislature)
  • Sir Owen Dixon: 'strict legalism'

Critiques / Realism

  • Multiple laws may apply to same facts
  • Laws inevitably over/underinclusive
  • Judges have ideology (unconscious)
  • Can lead to absurd/unjust results
  • Morals and society evolve
  • Hides judicial choice behind 'constraint'

Liberal Feminism: Gender equality possible within existing system. Remove legal barriers. Formal equality. Wollstonecraft.
Radical (MacKinnon): Law created by males in their image. Not about gender difference but DOMINANCE. Women = embodiments of men's sexual needs.
Cultural/Difference (Gilligan): Women have different ethical framework (care vs justice). Different but not inferior. Criticised as reinforcing stereotypes.
Socialist: Gendered division of labour supports capitalism. Gender inequality rooted in economic structure. Engels: capitalism defeated 'mother right'.
Postmodern: Rejects grand narratives. Particular instances of oppression. Acknowledges intersectionality. Challenges legal rhetoric.

Hart's Compromise: Minimum Content of Natural Law
Law must align with superior moral principles. Immoral law is not law at all. Finnis: 7 basic goods provide foundation for moral evaluation of law.
Law is law once properly enacted. No necessary connection to morality. Austin: existence of law is one thing, its merit another. Focus on social facts.
Hart: Law and morality are separate BUT law must have 'minimum content' for survival (don't kill, don't steal). Without this, no reason to obey. Moral evaluation still separate from legal validity.

1st Gen: Right to life, liberty, fair trial, vote, free speech, religion. Negative rights (state must NOT interfere). ICCPR. Liberal foundations.
2nd Gen: Right to education, health, housing, work, social security. Positive rights (state MUST provide). ICESCR. Socialist influence.
3rd Gen: Right to self-determination, development, clean environment, peace. Collective rights of peoples. UNDRIP. Emerging.

📚 Theme 1: Rule of Law & Formalism

Every exam
Dicey
Thin v Thick
Shklar quote

Past Questions:

"It would not be very difficult to show that the phrase 'the rule of law' has become meaningless thanks to ideological abuse and general over-use." Analyse with reference to legal formalism. (2018 T2 Q5)
Is there a difference between the rule of law and rule by law? Explore how legal formalism may play a role. (2019 T3 Q3)
To what extent is it correct that the rule of law 'forms an assumption' under which the Australian Constitution is framed? (2019 T3 Q4)
What are the advantages and disadvantages of legal formalism? (2018 T1 Part II)

Key Points to Cover:

• Dicey's 3 principles (supremacy of law, equality before law, rights as consequence not source)
• Formal/thin (Raz) v substantive/thick (Fuller, Bingham) conceptions
• Formal legality: general, promulgated, clear, specific, prospective, practicable, stable
• Constitutional legality: separation of powers, independent judiciary
• Legal formalism: judges apply rules without external considerations
• Formalism pros (objectivity, separation of powers) v cons (absurd results, blind to context)
• Cases: Entick v Carrington, A v Hayden, Haneef v Commonwealth

📚 Theme 2: Human Rights & Bill of Rights

Every exam
ICCPR
Constitutional rights
ACT/Vic charters

Past Questions:

Critically explore the need for a legal instrument at the Australian federal level that codifies human rights protections. Consider the manner of implementation. (2018 T2 Q1)
Critically reflect on arguments by Australian proponents of constitutional incorporation of human rights protections. (2019 T3 Q5)
Should Australia introduce a Bill of Rights? If so, should it be entrenched in the Constitution? (Exam summary note)

Key Points to Cover:

• Australia: only common law democracy without Bill of Rights
• Express constitutional rights: s51(xxxi) just terms, s80 jury trial, s116 religion, s117 non-discrimination
• Implied freedom of political communication
• Common law protections (principle of legality, Coco v The Queen)
• ACT Human Rights Act 2004, Victorian Charter 2006 (dialogue model)
• ICCPR, ICESCR ratified but not fully implemented domestically
• For: comprehensive protection, educative role, accountability, international obligations
• Against: judicial imperialism, parliamentary sovereignty, against tradition, inflexible
• Generations: 1st (civil/political), 2nd (economic/social/cultural), 3rd (collective/solidarity)

📚 Theme 3: Aboriginal Law & Terra Nullius

Every exam
Mabo
R v Murrell
Walker

Past Questions:

Critically examine historical and contemporary arguments for and against recognition of Aboriginal law. (2019 T3 Q2)
In Walker v NSW, Mason CJ stated all people should stand equal before the law. Critically analyse with reference to recognition of Aboriginal law. (2018 T2 Q2)
Critically analyse arguments in R v Murrell and other cases on whether Aboriginal people are subject to Australian criminal law. (2018 T2 Q4)
Why did colonial powers decide Australia was terra nullius? Relationship to law of nations? (2017 Part B Q9)

Key Points to Cover:

• Terra nullius: land belonging to no one, justified settlement without treaty
• De Vattel's Law of Nations: obligation to cultivate earth
• R v Ballard (1829): no jurisdiction over Aboriginal-on-Aboriginal crime
• R v Murrell (1836): reversed Ballard, English law applies to all
• R v Bonjon (1841): Willis J questioned jurisdiction (removed from bench)
• Mabo v QLD No 2 (1992): rejected terra nullius as 'false in fact', recognised native title
• Walker v NSW (1994): criminal law universal, equality before law
• ALRC: functional recognition, community level, unless repugnant to CL/HR
• Arguments for: self-determination, cultural preservation, UNDRIP
• Arguments against: equality before law, legal centralism, jurisdictional issues
• Indigenous sentencing courts (circle courts), restorative justice

📚 Theme 4: Feminism & Gender

Most exams
3 waves/phases
Public/private
MacKinnon

Past Questions:

Critically consider the role of feminist jurisprudence, including Catharine MacKinnon, in the advancement of gender equality. (2019 T3 Q7)
Kate Jenkins (2017) statement on progress but incomplete gender equality. Critically analyse using theories of feminism. (2018 T2 Q7)
Are liberalism and feminism compatible? (2018 T1 Part II)
Carol Smart: 'the engulfing embrace of liberalism has done so little to emancipate women.' Why? (2017 Q8)

Key Points to Cover:

• Naffine's 3 phases: (1) Male Monopoly/formal equality (2) Male Culture/substantive equality (3) Legal Rhetoric/post-structuralist
• Public/private dichotomy: women's oppression in private sphere, limited state intervention
• MacKinnon: dominance theory, law reflects male power, not neutral
• Carol Smart: law as gendered, not just biased
• Sex Discrimination Act 1984, Fair Work Act 2009
• Formal equality limits: benchmark is white middle-class male
• Ongoing issues: pay gap, domestic violence, representation

📚 Theme 5: Globalisation & Law

Most exams
Twining
Terrorism
MNCs

Past Questions:

William Twining writes that forces of globalisation have a profound impact on domestic legal orders. To what extent is this true of Australia? (2019 T3 Q6)
To what extent is state sovereignty influenced by processes of globalisation? (2018 T2 Q6)
What was Australia's legal response to terrorism post-globalisation? What is problematic from a rule of law perspective? (Exam summary note)

Key Points to Cover:

• Twining: nation states not fully in charge of own legal agendas
• Globalised localism v localised globalism (De Sousa Santos)
• Globalisation OF law (unification) v BY law (international agreements changing domestic law)
• Dualist system: treaties need domestic legislation to have effect
• Mabo: international law as 'legitimate and important influence' on common law
• Counter-terrorism: Criminal Code s101.1, ancillary offences, 14-day detention
• Rule of law concerns: no harm principle breached, preventative detention, Haneef case
• MNCs: accountability gaps, corporate governance export, Modern Slavery Act

📚 Theme 6: Equality

Most exams
Fredman
Formal v Substantive

Past Questions:

'Law is, and should only be, concerned with formal equality before the law.' Critically analyse with emphasis on achievement of justice. (2019 T3 Q1)
What is the difference between positive and negative liberty? (2018 T1 Part II)

Key Points to Cover:

• Formal equality: equal treatment, absence of barriers, treats likes alike
• Substantive equality: equal outcomes, affirmative action, addresses systemic disadvantage
• Fredman's 4 dimensions: redress disadvantage, counter prejudice, enhance voice/participation, accommodate difference
• Anti-discrimination legislation: RDA 1975, SDA 1984, DDA 1992, ADA 2004
• Positive liberty (freedom TO) v negative liberty (freedom FROM)
• Tension: individualism v collective welfare
• Benchmark problem: comparator is dominant group norm

🎯 Exam MCQ Practice (from 2018 T1)

A. What types of actions would Kant admit as moral?
→ d) None of the above (Kant requires duty-based action, not lying for any reason)

B. Virginia School of public choice theory: actions motivated by?
→ c) Self interest

C. What is included in the THIN version of Rule of Law?
→ d) None of the above (market economy, welfare, democratic governance are all thick/substantive)

D. Power to interpret the Constitution rests with?
→ b) The High Court

E. Wheelchair access for university students is an example of?
→ a) Substantive equality

F. In Mabo, terra nullius was described as?
→ b) False in fact

G. Implied right found in Constitution's language/structure?
→ a) Freedom of political debate

✅ Exam Strategy Tips

1. Time management: For 3-essay exams, spend 15 min planning + 40 min per essay. For mixed formats, allocate proportionally by marks.

2. Structure essays: Introduction (define key terms, state position) → Body (use cases + theory, address both sides) → Conclusion (take a stance)

3. Use cases: Every essay should reference at least 2-3 cases. The markers love: Mabo, Coco v The Queen, A v Hayden, Walker v NSW, Entick v Carrington, Donoghue v Stevenson.

4. Name theorists: Dicey, Raz, Fuller, Mill, Locke, Bentham, Rawls, Nozick, Fredman, MacKinnon, Twining, Shklar.

5. Closed book prep: Memorise key case names + 1-line ratios, key section numbers (s51, s72, s80, s92, s116, s117, s128), and theorist names + core ideas.
